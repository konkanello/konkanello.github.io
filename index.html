Bio

I am a computer architect. I recently completed my PhD at ETH Zurich (defended May 2026), advised by Prof. Onur Mutlu in the SAFARI Research Group. Before that, I received my Diploma (5-year integrated Master's) in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the National Technical University of Athens (NTUA) in Greece. My work has appeared at venues such as MICRO, ISCA, ASPLOS, HPCA, and DSN, and some of it has received best-paper and artifact recognitions.

Research vision. My research is driven by a deep interest in application–OS–hardware co-design, which I believe offers a powerful path toward building high-performance, energy-efficient, programmable, and predictable computer systems. I am particularly interested in rethinking how applications, operating systems, and hardware work together, rather than optimizing each layer in isolation. I currently focus on three closely connected challenges: (i) redesigning how computers manage memory so that modern data-intensive applications execute efficiently without giving up compatibility or ease of programming; (ii) rethinking processor design for the AI era, where modern workloads increasingly rely on the CPU and operating system for critical services such as memory management, scheduling, and communication; and (iii) building the systems foundations for Physical AI, where computers control robots and other machines in the real world and where timely, predictable execution is essential for safety.

On the academic job market. I am applying for tenure-track faculty positions in the 2026/2027 cycle. If you think there might be a good fit at your department, or you'd simply like to chat about my research, please reach out at konkanello@gmail.com. I would love to hear from you.

Beyond research. In my free time, I really enjoy cooking, watching movies, playing tennis (and sometimes padel), and appreciating or practicing forms of art on the electric guitar and the piano. Lately, I relate a lot to this poem by Cavafy, “Ithaka.”

Ithaka by Cavafy
Ελληνικά Original
Σα βγεις στον πηγαιμό για την Ιθάκη, να εύχεσαι νά ’ναι μακρύς ο δρόμος, γεμάτος περιπέτειες, γεμάτος γνώσεις. Τους Λαιστρυγόνας και τους Κύκλωπας, τον θυμωμένο Ποσειδώνα μη φοβάσαι, τέτοια στον δρόμο σου ποτέ σου δεν θα βρεις, αν μέν’ η σκέψις σου υψηλή, αν εκλεκτή συγκίνησις το πνεύμα και το σώμα σου αγγίζει. Τους Λαιστρυγόνας και τους Κύκλωπας, τον άγριο Ποσειδώνα δεν θα συναντήσεις, αν δεν τους κουβανείς μες στην ψυχή σου, αν η ψυχή σου δεν τους στήνει εμπρός σου. Να εύχεσαι νά ’ναι μακρύς ο δρόμος. Πολλά τα καλοκαιρινά πρωιά να είναι που με τι ευχαρίστησι, με τι χαρά θα μπαίνεις σε λιμένας πρωτοειδωμένους· να σταματήσεις σ’ εμπορεία Φοινικικά, και τες καλές πραγμάτειες ν’ αποκτήσεις, σεντέφια και κοράλλια, κεχριμπάρια κ’ έβενους, και ηδονικά μυρωδικά κάθε λογής, όσο μπορείς πιο άφθονα ηδονικά μυρωδικά· σε πόλεις Αιγυπτιακές πολλές να πας, να μάθεις και να μάθεις απ’ τους σπουδασμένους. Πάντα στον νου σου νά ’χεις την Ιθάκη. Το φθάσιμον εκεί είν’ ο προορισμός σου. Αλλά μη βιάζεις το ταξείδι διόλου. Καλλίτερα χρόνια πολλά να διαρκέσει· και γέρος πια ν’ αράξεις στο νησί, πλούσιος με όσα κέρδισες στον δρόμο, μη προσδοκώντας πλούτη να σε δώσει η Ιθάκη. Η Ιθάκη σ’ έδωσε τ’ ωραίο ταξείδι. Χωρίς αυτήν δεν θά ’βγαινες στον δρόμο. Άλλα δεν έχει να σε δώσει πια. Κι αν πτωχική την βρεις, η Ιθάκη δεν σε γέλασε. Έτσι σοφός που έγινες, με τόση πείρα, ήδη θα το κατάλαβες οι Ιθάκες τι σημαίνουν.
English Rendering
When you set out on your journey to Ithaka, wish for the road to stretch far, rich in adventures, rich in things to learn. The Laestrygonians and the Cyclopes, angry Poseidon—do not fear them; you will never find such beings on your way if your thought remains lofty, if an exquisite feeling touches your spirit and body. The Laestrygonians and the Cyclopes, fierce Poseidon—you will not encounter them unless you carry them within your soul, unless your soul sets them before you. Wish for the road to stretch far. May there be many summer mornings when, with what pleasure, with what joy, you enter harbors seen for the first time; may you stop at Phoenician markets and acquire their fine wares: mother-of-pearl and coral, amber and ebony, and sensuous fragrances of every kind— as many sensuous fragrances as you can; may you visit many Egyptian cities to learn, and learn again, from the learned. Always keep Ithaka in your mind. To arrive there is your destination. But do not hurry the journey at all. Better for it to last many years, and for you, old by then, to anchor at the island, rich with all you gained along the way, with no expectation that Ithaka will grant you riches. Ithaka gave you the beautiful journey. Without her, you would never have taken to the road. She has nothing else to give you now. And if you find her poor, Ithaka did not deceive you. Wise as you have become, with so much experience, by then you will have understood what Ithakas mean.

Independent English rendering from Cavafy’s Greek original. Read the Keeley–Sherrard translation at Poetry Foundation.
